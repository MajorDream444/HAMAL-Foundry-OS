# Foundry Industrial Intelligence OS

## Qatar Incubator Workspace Charter

**Working status:** Concept and diligence stage  
**Prepared for:** Major Dream Williams / AMA Solutions  
**Core platform:** Foundry OS  
**First vertical:** Process safety and regulated industrial operations  
**Reference integration:** Mach II Digital Engineer, subject to written rights and technical diligence

## 1. Strategic thesis

Foundry Industrial Intelligence OS is a governed operating layer for high-risk, highly regulated industries. It connects specialist engineering systems, facility records, standards, people, approvals, remediation tasks and audit evidence into one traceable intelligence environment.

The first vertical focuses on oil, gas, petrochemical and process-safety operations. Mach II provides a strong example of the specialist calculation and documentation engine that can sit beneath Foundry. Foundry provides the intelligence, governance, workflow, evidence and organizational layer around it.

The venture should not be presented as ownership of Mach II or its underlying methodology unless a written licence, integration agreement or joint-venture agreement grants those rights.

## 2. Why this belongs in a separate workspace

This venture is distinct from HAMAL BET and the Namibia gaming opportunity:

| Dimension | Foundry Industrial Intelligence | HAMAL BET |
|---|---|---|
| Primary sector | Energy, petrochemicals and regulated industry | Gaming and retail sportsbook operations |
| Primary buyer | Industrial operator, EPC, regulator or asset owner | Licensed gaming operator |
| Core risk | Engineering safety, process integrity and auditability | Cash custody, gaming exposure and player protection |
| Specialist engine | Engineering and compliance systems such as Mach II | Odds, tickets, settlement and retail terminal systems |
| Foundry role | Evidence, approvals, remediation and intelligence | Retail operations, risk, cash and compliance intelligence |

The shared asset is Foundry OS. The industries, partners, liability models and commercial structures remain separate.

## 3. Incubator-ready problem statement

Industrial facilities depend on engineering calculations, standards, inspection records, maintenance systems, operator knowledge and regulatory evidence that frequently live in disconnected software, documents and individual experts' heads. This fragmentation makes it difficult to determine:

- Which assets are safe and compliant;
- Which engineering assumptions are current;
- Who approved a critical decision;
- Which corrective actions remain open;
- Whether evidence is ready for an audit;
- Where workforce knowledge is missing;
- Whether the same risk is repeating across multiple facilities.

Foundry turns these disconnected records and specialist tools into a governed, human-authorized operating system.

## 4. Product architecture

### Layer 1 — Specialist engineering systems

- Relief-system calculations;
- Process-safety models;
- Inspection and maintenance systems;
- Sensor and operational data;
- Engineering drawings and specifications;
- Standards, procedures and facility records.

Mach II may serve as one specialist engine in this layer. Foundry does not replace engineering judgment.

### Layer 2 — Foundry evidence graph

- Source provenance;
- Requirement-to-evidence mapping;
- Version and change history;
- Asset, facility, person and document relationships;
- Calculation inputs and output lineage;
- Confidence and validation status.

### Layer 3 — Governed intelligence

- AI-assisted document and data review;
- Missing-evidence detection;
- Contradiction and stale-record alerts;
- Risk summaries;
- Standards cross-referencing;
- Human review and approval gates.

### Layer 4 — Operational workflow

- Corrective-action routing;
- Work orders and ownership;
- Escalations and deadlines;
- Training and competency requirements;
- Contractor and supplier coordination;
- Management-of-change workflows.

### Layer 5 — Assurance and reporting

- Facility dashboards;
- Audit-ready evidence packages;
- Management reports;
- Regulator-facing exports;
- Multi-site risk views;
- Immutable decision and approval records.

## 5. The Qatar story

Qatar is a logical launch environment because the proposition aligns with complex energy and industrial infrastructure, process safety, operational excellence, local capability development and sovereign control of industrial data.

The incubator narrative should be:

> Qatar can become the launch market for a sovereign industrial intelligence platform that helps operators connect engineering truth, workforce action and regulatory evidence without surrendering control of sensitive facility data.

The pitch must not claim that Mach II represents a new worldwide regulation. The defensible position is that Foundry helps organizations operationalize applicable laws, standards and recognized engineering practices across jurisdictions.

## 6. Incubator MVP

The MVP should prove one complete operating loop rather than attempt to replace every industrial system.

### Recommended demonstration

1. Ingest one facility's relief-system design package;
2. Link calculations, drawings, equipment tags and governing requirements;
3. Display the evidence and approval status for each protected asset;
4. Detect missing, conflicting or outdated information;
5. Route one corrective action to a responsible engineer;
6. Require authorized human approval;
7. generate an audit-ready evidence package;
8. preserve the complete decision trail.

### Five MVP screens

1. **Facility Command Center** — assets, current risk and unresolved actions.
2. **Engineering Evidence View** — inputs, calculations, sources, versions and approvals.
3. **Risk and Exception Queue** — missing evidence, conflicts and overdue remediation.
4. **Human Authorization Console** — review, approve, reject, annotate and escalate.
5. **Audit Pack Builder** — select scope and export the supporting evidence trail.

## 7. Ownership and partnership boundary

Before Mach II appears by name in an incubator submission, obtain written answers to the following:

1. Who owns the software, trademarks, source code and documentation?
2. Who has authority to grant API, integration and demonstration rights?
3. May AMA/HAMAL use the name and screenshots in an incubator application?
4. May Foundry ingest Mach II files and outputs?
5. Is there an API or only file-based interoperability?
6. Can the technology be deployed in Qatar and the Gulf?
7. Who bears professional-engineering and product liability?
8. What validation has been independently reviewed?
9. What AIChE/DIERS marks or materials are licensed for commercial use?
10. What are the licence, integration, subscription and reseller economics?

Until these are answered, the incubator application should describe Mach II as a **potential specialist integration**, not a HAMAL-owned product.

## 8. Commercial model

The proposed Foundry business model has four possible revenue layers:

- Initial facility assessment and implementation;
- Platform licence or subscription;
- Specialist-system integrations;
- Ongoing intelligence, assurance and audit support.

Optional services may include training, data remediation, standards mapping, multi-site rollout and local workforce development.

Mach II licensing or referral commissions should remain contractually separate from Foundry platform revenue.

## 9. Evidence discipline

Every major statement in the application should carry one of five internal labels:

- **VERIFIED** — independently supported;
- **REPORTED** — stated by a party or document but not independently verified;
- **PROPOSAL** — a future design or commercial structure;
- **OPEN** — information still required;
- **CONFLICT** — sources disagree.

This protects the incubator application from turning partner claims into HAMAL claims.

## 12. Immediate decision

The project should proceed as a Foundry OS venture with a process-safety vertical. Mach II is potentially a powerful first integration and evidence set, but Foundry must remain technically useful and commercially viable even if Mach II integration rights are not granted.

That independence is what makes the concept incubator-ready, investable and scalable beyond a single partner.
