# Foundry OS Qatar: Needs Landscape and Opportunity Ranking

**Status:** Research sprint v1  
**Purpose:** Decide which Qatar problem Foundry OS should validate before a Startup Qatar application  
**Evidence labels:** VERIFIED, REPORTED, INFERENCE, PROPOSAL, OPEN, CONFLICT

## Executive decision

**Primary wedge: Capability-to-Contract for SME supplier readiness.** Build a governed execution layer that helps a sponsor move selected SMEs from a diagnosed capability gap to buyer-accepted evidence of operational improvement. The first version should focus on general supplier readiness and digital/operational maturity, not official ICV certification and not regulated engineering decisions.

**Secondary wedge: Capability-to-Role for AI-enabled workforce readiness.** Help an employer, university or workforce program validate people through real assignments, governed AI use, supervisor approval and portable work evidence.

**Do not lead with:** a generic LMS, job board, talent marketplace, accelerator dashboard, official ICV scoring product, regulated FinTech, gaming, industrial-safety automation or Mach II integration.

**Application route:** AMA should presently apply under **START** with Foundry OS as a new product from an experienced operating and technical team. A Hanzo-led **GROW** application is not supportable from public or supplied evidence alone. It becomes supportable only if Hanzo is the actual applicant, has at least three years of qualifying operations, can document financial stability and funding history, controls or licenses all product IP used by Foundry OS, and can explain why the Qatar expansion belongs commercially inside Hanzo rather than AMA. The official program defines START and GROW at the applying-startup level, not at the level of an affiliated partner's history.

## Qatar's ten most important addressable problems

### 1. Training does not automatically prove work readiness

**Evidence:** VERIFIED national targets emphasize highly skilled labor, skills-based recruitment, productivity and comprehensive training. Existing platforms match people to roles, but the public material does not show a shared system for real-work trials, governed AI usage, supervisor receipts and portable performance evidence.

**Affected users:** students, recent graduates, Qataris entering the private sector, expatriate graduates, career changers, hiring managers and supervisors.  
**Potential buyers:** Ministry of Labour programs, universities, Qatar Foundation entities, large employers, sector academies.  
**Budget owners:** HR, learning and development, workforce transformation, graduate programs, Qatarization offices.  
**Existing responses:** Ouqoul, Basher, labour re-employment platform, Istamer, Qatar Career Development Center, university career services, employer training programs.  
**Unresolved:** **INFERENCE** - matching and course completion do not provide consistent evidence that a candidate can perform a role with AI under accountable supervision.  
**Foundry relevance:** Very high, but employer acceptance and integration requirements are OPEN.

### 2. Qatari private-sector participation and retention remain structurally difficult

**Evidence:** VERIFIED policy seeks increased Qatari participation and private-sector employment; an ILO/QFC skills study reports that only 30% of unemployed Qataris in the 2024 Labour Force Survey were willing to work in the private sector, primarily because of wage differences. The IMF has also identified the concentration of nationals in public employment as a structural issue. [ILO/QFC financial-sector skills study](https://www.ilo.org/publications/identifying-skills-needs-qatars-financial-sector-qfc-case-study), [IMF Qatar Selected Issues](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/002/2024/011/article-A001-en.xml)

**Affected users:** Qatari graduates, private employers, HR teams and line managers.  
**Potential buyers:** large private companies, sector employers, workforce-policy programs.  
**Budget owners:** HR, Qatarization, talent, compensation, workforce strategy.  
**Existing responses:** Qatarization law and programs, private-sector hiring platforms, employer incentives and career guidance.  
**Unresolved:** Foundry can improve readiness and progression evidence but cannot solve public/private wage and benefit differences.  
**Foundry relevance:** Medium. Reject any claim that software alone solves Qatarization.

### 3. SMEs struggle to translate supplier requirements into operational proof

**Evidence:** VERIFIED Tawteen supports energy-sector supplier development, while ICV certification requires evidence-based scorecards and supporting documents validated by approved certifiers. QDB separately publishes resilience and ESG guidance for SMEs.  
**Affected users:** SME owners, operations leaders, finance teams, quality teams and employees.  
**Potential buyers:** supplier-development sponsors, anchor enterprises, economic-development institutions and SME-support programs.  
**Budget owners:** procurement, supply-chain localization, supplier quality, local content, enterprise development.  
**Existing responses:** Tawteen, approved ICV certifiers, QDB advisory/finance, ERP and quality consultants.  
**Unresolved:** **INFERENCE** - SMEs may understand requirements but lack a day-to-day system to assign remediation work, build staff capability and assemble buyer-ready evidence.  
**Foundry relevance:** Highest commercial fit. It has a buyer, measurable workflow and implementation budget, subject to sponsor confirmation.

### 4. SME digital transformation is fragmented between advice, technology and adoption

**Evidence:** VERIFIED QDB and national programs promote Industry 4.0, digital transformation and business resilience. QDB guidance explicitly addresses supply-chain disruption, liquidity, risk, logistics and human-capital productivity. [QDB Industry 4.0 initiative](https://www.qdb.qa/about/news/news/towards-digital-transformation), [QDB Resources](https://www.qdb.qa/insights-and-publications/resources-and-manuals)

**Affected users:** SME owners, operations teams and frontline staff.  
**Potential buyers:** QDB-style SME programs, banks, chambers, anchor buyers and sector sponsors.  
**Budget owners:** digital transformation, SME development, operations, corporate banking/SME support.  
**Existing responses:** consultants, software vendors, QDB programs, incubators and training providers.  
**Unresolved:** **OPEN** - whether sponsors lack implementation visibility after assessment and training; whether SMEs will share operating data; whether they will pay directly.  
**Foundry relevance:** High when tied to a specific buyer requirement; weak as generic transformation consulting.

### 5. Incubators fund and train startups but cannot guarantee execution or adoption

**Evidence:** VERIFIED Qatar has extensive incubation, funding, mentoring, R&D and market-adoption programs through Startup Qatar, QSTP, QDB/QBIC and others. QSTP's program stack spans exploration, incubation, acceleration and scaling. [QSTP](https://qstp.qa/), [Startup Qatar Investment Program](https://www.qdb.qa/Financing-And-Funding/Equity-And-Investment/Startup-Qatar-Investment-Program)

**Affected users:** founders, accelerator managers, mentors, investors and corporate pilot owners.  
**Potential buyers:** incubators, venture studios, university innovation programs, corporate innovation teams.  
**Budget owners:** program management, innovation, portfolio support, investment teams.  
**Existing responses:** accelerator-management software, CRM/project tools, mentor reporting and data rooms.  
**Unresolved:** **INFERENCE** - public program breadth does not prove a gap in portfolio execution. Foundry must show something materially different: governed agents, accepted work evidence, decision receipts and team contribution records.  
**Foundry relevance:** Good pilot access, but high risk of appearing like ordinary accelerator software.

### 6. Skills and performance evidence are fragmented across institutions

**Evidence:** VERIFIED Qatar operates multiple specialized workforce platforms and institutional programs. **INFERENCE:** each solves a stage or constituency; public sources do not show a common, portable evidence layer linking learning, real work, supervisor acceptance and progression across institutions.

**Affected users:** candidates, institutions, employers and program administrators.  
**Potential buyers:** a consortium sponsor, large employer, university network or workforce authority.  
**Budget owners:** digital transformation, HR technology, workforce policy.  
**Existing responses:** HRIS/LMS platforms, recruitment portals, digital credentials and government matching systems.  
**Unresolved:** data-sharing authority, identity architecture, consent, standards and institutional willingness.  
**Foundry relevance:** Strategically powerful but too integration-heavy for the first 90-day commercial wedge.

### 7. Logistics digitization requires SME and workforce adoption, not only infrastructure

**Evidence:** VERIFIED MCIT and the Ministry of Transport launched a transport and logistics digital-transformation roadmap as part of a national effort in priority sectors. NDS3 seeks to make Qatar a global logistics hub. [MCIT logistics roadmap](https://www.mcit.gov.qa/en/news/mcit-and-ministry-of-transport-launch-transport-and-logistics-digital-transformation-roadmap-2), [NDS3](https://www.npc.qa/en/planning/nds3/Documents/QNDS3_EN.pdf)

**Affected users:** logistics SMEs, freight forwarders, warehouse teams, transport operators and supervisors.  
**Potential buyers:** large logistics operators, free zones, ports/airport ecosystem actors, SME-support sponsors.  
**Budget owners:** operations, supply chain, digital transformation, vendor development.  
**Existing responses:** transport-management systems, warehouse systems, sector roadmap projects, integrators and training.  
**Unresolved:** which operator has a specific supplier/workforce adoption failure and will provide real assignments/data.  
**Foundry relevance:** Best first vertical sandbox if a sponsor is secured; the vertical is not itself the product.

### 8. Enterprise AI adoption needs governed human authorization and auditable use

**Evidence:** VERIFIED Digital Agenda 2030 and the Government AI program create strong institutional demand for digital and AI capability. **INFERENCE:** wider deployment increases the need for role permissions, approved workflows, human decisions and auditable outputs. [MCIT Digital Agenda 2030](https://www.mcit.gov.qa/en/digital-agenda-2030), [MCIT GovAI Program](https://www.mcit.gov.qa/en/gov-ai-program)

**Affected users:** employees, supervisors, risk teams, IT and compliance.  
**Potential buyers:** government entities, regulated enterprises, major employers and education providers.  
**Budget owners:** CIO, CDO, AI office, information security, risk, HR transformation.  
**Existing responses:** enterprise AI platforms, hyperscalers, consulting firms, GRC systems and internal policies.  
**Unresolved:** Foundry's technical controls, hosting, Arabic support, security certifications and integration advantages are not yet proven.  
**Foundry relevance:** Core enabling capability, not a sufficiently narrow initial sales proposition.

### 9. Research and startup programs still need repeatable commercialization pathways

**Evidence:** VERIFIED QSTP explicitly supports incubation, acceleration, enterprise engagement, a Tech Venture Fund and commercialization; its scale shows a developed ecosystem. [QSTP](https://qstp.qa/)  
**Affected users:** researchers, technical founders, university spinouts, commercialization teams and enterprise adopters.  
**Potential buyers:** QSTP-like institutions, universities, research centers and corporate innovation programs.  
**Budget owners:** technology transfer, innovation, R&D commercialization, venture investment.  
**Existing responses:** QSTP, QDB, Startup Qatar, grants, venture funds, corporate innovation programs.  
**Unresolved:** **OPEN** - no primary evidence reviewed proves that Qatar needs another commercialization platform. Buyer interviews must identify a concrete failure, such as slow pilot conversion, missing evidence or weak founder operating capacity.  
**Foundry relevance:** Secondary only.

### 10. Energy-sector localization requires capability transfer beyond certification

**Evidence:** VERIFIED Tawteen is QatarEnergy's supply-chain localization program and operates supplier development and evidence-based ICV certification. [QatarEnergy sustainability/localization](https://www.qatarenergy.qa/en/Sustainability/Pages/SocialandEconomicDevelopment.aspx), [Tawteen](https://www.tawteen.com.qa/)  
**Affected users:** local energy suppliers, technicians, engineers, quality teams and procurement teams.  
**Potential buyers:** QatarEnergy ecosystem companies, EPCs and supplier-development sponsors.  
**Budget owners:** localization, procurement, supplier quality, operations, technical training.  
**Existing responses:** Tawteen, ICV certifiers, enterprise qualification systems, technical academies and specialist consultants.  
**Unresolved:** access is relationship-heavy; liability and qualification standards are high; incumbent systems are substantial. Mach II rights are unverified and do not establish Qatar demand.  
**Foundry relevance:** High-value expansion wedge after a lower-risk supplier-readiness pilot.

## Recommended pilot: Capability-to-Contract Lab

### Pilot customer and users

**Sponsor:** one anchor enterprise, supplier-development program, economic-development institution or sector operator.  
**Users:** 6-10 SMEs, 15-30 SME employees/operators, 2-4 sponsor reviewers, 2 Foundry program operators.  
**Vertical:** logistics, professional services or general industrial support. Avoid regulated engineering decisions in pilot one.

### Twelve-week workflow

1. Sponsor defines 3-5 readiness standards and supplies sanitized evidence requirements.
2. SMEs complete baseline capability, process and evidence assessments.
3. Foundry converts gaps into role-specific learning and real operating tasks.
4. Governed agents assist research, documentation, analysis and workflow execution.
5. Named SME supervisors approve internal work; sponsor reviewers accept or reject milestone evidence.
6. Foundry issues no official certification. It produces a sponsor-specific readiness record and evidence room.
7. Each SME receives a next-stage action plan; qualified firms may be considered by the sponsor under the sponsor's own procurement rules.

### Deliverables

- sponsor requirements and evidence map;
- SME baseline and prioritized gap plan;
- participant skill/work passports;
- governed task workbench with approval receipts;
- evidence room for each SME;
- sponsor dashboard for milestones, exceptions and acceptance;
- final readiness report and replication template.

### KPIs

- percentage of assigned remediation tasks accepted by sponsor reviewers;
- median time from identified gap to accepted evidence;
- number of operational gaps closed;
- first-pass acceptance rate of work products;
- active participant completion and supervisor approval rates;
- hours saved in sponsor follow-up/document chasing;
- SME user satisfaction and sponsor willingness to continue;
- number of SMEs invited to a next procurement/qualification step - measured without promising contracts.

### Pricing logic

**PROPOSAL:** price the first pilot as institutional implementation, not per learner.

- Discovery and configuration: **QAR 90,000-150,000**.
- Twelve-week cohort implementation: **QAR 180,000-320,000**.
- Total pilot range: **QAR 270,000-470,000**, excluding bespoke integrations, security certification and travel.
- Post-pilot platform: annual institutional license plus per-program implementation/support. Final pricing requires interviews and delivery costing.

The sponsor should pay. SMEs may participate at no cost or co-pay only if the sponsor believes payment improves commitment. Foundry should not depend on cash-constrained SMEs as the first buyer.

### Required local partners

- a sponsor with real supplier or workforce requirements;
- a Qatar-licensed commercial/legal adviser for contracting and data issues;
- an Arabic-speaking program lead;
- a local university, incubator or SME network for recruitment;
- approved ICV or sector specialists only when their authority is relevant;
- Qatar-hosting/security partner if customer policy requires local hosting.

## START versus GROW

The official Startup Qatar page states:

- START supports innovative startups launching in Qatar and requires an unmet need, validated concept, technology roadmap, market research/readiness, business model, monetization strategy and experienced committed leadership.
- GROW supports established startups expanding into Qatar and requires at least three years of operations, qualified management, positive growth outlook, visible financial stability and adequate funding history. [QDB Startup Qatar Investment Program](https://www.qdb.qa/Financing-And-Funding/Equity-And-Investment/Startup-Qatar-Investment-Program)

### Decision

**AMA + Foundry OS: START.** AMA's history and Hanzo's technical history strengthen team credibility but do not transform a new product into a three-year operating startup. The application story is honest and strong: experienced operators are launching a validated new B2B SaaS product in Qatar.

**Hanzo-led GROW: OPEN, currently unsupported.** It requires documentary diligence on:

1. applying legal entity and jurisdiction;
2. three complete years of qualifying operations;
3. audited or otherwise accepted evidence of financial stability;
4. funding history;
5. management authority and commitment to Qatar;
6. product/IP ownership and AMA's rights;
7. commercial rationale for Foundry OS as a Hanzo expansion;
8. cap-table and funding implications for Major Dream Williams and AMA.

Even if Hanzo qualifies, GROW may weaken Foundry's ownership clarity and AMA's strategic control. Eligibility is not the only decision criterion.

## Claims requiring interviews or written verification

1. A Qatar institution will pay for work-readiness evidence rather than training alone.
2. Sponsor reviewers will accept Foundry-generated evidence rooms in their existing qualification process.
3. Supplier-development teams have material costs from document chasing, remediation visibility or inconsistent SME execution.
4. Six to ten SMEs can share sufficient operating data for a 12-week pilot.
5. Logistics or professional services is the best first vertical.
6. The sponsor budget sits with procurement/supplier development rather than HR, IT or economic development.
7. Qatar hosting is mandatory for the intended data class.
8. Arabic user experience is mandatory at pilot stage rather than scale stage.
9. Existing sponsor systems lack task-level evidence and human-approval receipts.
10. Institutions will recognize a portable participant work passport.
11. The proposed price range fits procurement thresholds and comparable program budgets.
12. AMA has the rights needed to present and commercialize Hanzo and Lux capabilities inside Foundry OS.
13. Hanzo's operating, financial and funding history satisfies GROW requirements.
14. One or more institutions will issue a pilot expression of interest before application.
15. Any Mach II naming, integration, demonstration or commercialization rights are documented.

## Immediate discovery sequence

Conduct 15 interviews in three groups:

- **5 anchor buyers:** procurement/localization, supplier quality, workforce transformation or innovation leaders.
- **5 SMEs/startups:** owners and operations leads who have completed training, certification or incubation but still struggle to win or deliver contracts.
- **5 ecosystem operators:** QDB/QSTP-style program staff, university career/innovation teams, certifiers and local implementation partners.

The PASS gate for the primary wedge is: two buyers describe the same costly workflow failure, one identifies an accessible budget, and one is willing to scope a supervised pilot using real requirements. Otherwise HOLD or REDIRECT before building beyond the clickable prototype.

## Supporting-material boundary

The supplied Foundry, mobility and Mach II materials demonstrate useful design patterns: evidence graphs, human authorization, staged capability progression, corrective-action routing and paths from operator to owner. They do **not** prove Qatar customer demand. Mach II remains a possible later specialist integration subject to written IP, demonstration, API, liability and commercialization rights.

## Technical reference assessment: a future Foundry World Qatar

### Decision

**PROPOSAL:** Use **Foundry World: Qatar** as a working name for a future executive intelligence surface and **Qatar Capability Twin** as a functional descriptor. Neither is the initial product wedge. The first product remains Foundry OS Capability-to-Contract; spatial intelligence is an optional presentation and decision-support layer.

The spatial layer passes the product test only if a sponsor must compare capability, interventions or outcomes across multiple sites, cohorts, supplier clusters or project locations. If the pilot can be understood better in a conventional portfolio table and evidence room, ship that first.

### System boundaries

| Layer | Authoritative role | Must not become |
|---|---|---|
| Foundry OS | Operational system of record for participants, SMEs, requirements, assignments, evidence, approvals, readiness states and outcomes | A decorative map database or an ungoverned data lake |
| MobStack | Workflow orchestration, task routing, permissioned approvals, escalation and human decision receipts | A substitute for buyer policy or legal authority |
| HAMAL | Governed intelligence layer for retrieval, recommendations, agents, policy checks and executive synthesis | An autonomous decision-maker for hiring, procurement or eligibility |
| Hanzo World | Read-only or tightly controlled executive intelligence surface combining maps, widgets, time and narrative | The source of record or the product thesis |
| Spatial visualization engine | Reusable rendering, camera, layers, filters, time playback and cinematic explanation derived from the reviewed repository | A surveillance console, worker tracker or military-styled interface |
| Lux | Optional provenance or settlement for a narrow, justified event class | A blockchain wrapper around every record or a claim of legal ownership |

**VERIFIED:** The public [Hanzo World](https://world.hanzo.ai/) interface currently demonstrates a modular, resizable executive dashboard with global/region selection, map-backed layouts, live and historical modes, export, search, monitoring, an AI analyst, organization-scoped analytics and operational widgets. This makes it relevant as a surface-pattern reference. It does not establish that its present data model, security controls or deployment architecture meet a Qatar customer's requirements.

**OPEN:** The Hanzo World page identifies its repository as `hanzoai/world`, but that repository was not publicly readable during this review. Reuse rights, internal dependencies, API contracts, tenant isolation and deployment rights therefore require Hanzo's written technical and commercial confirmation.

### God's Eye View repository: what is reusable

**VERIFIED:** The reviewed [MajorDream444 fork](https://github.com/MajorDream444/gods-eye-view) points back to Bilawal Sidhu's upstream project. Its code is MIT-licensed, and `package.json` identifies a browser stack centered on Vite and Cesium, with `@mapbox/vector-tile`, PBF parsing, MGRS, `satellite.js`, Puppeteer and Sharp. The application exposes modular startup phases for scene, controls, data and tools; a Cesium viewer module; distinct data/layer modules; shareable scenes; drawing/annotation; time and camera controls; and provider adapters.

**PROPOSAL — reuse candidates:**

- Cesium globe/viewer initialization and camera navigation;
- modular layer registration and visibility controls;
- GeoJSON/vector-tile ingestion for authorized aggregate layers;
- region filters, scene presets and time playback;
- annotations, project boundaries and site markers;
- shareable, permission-checked executive views;
- cinematic camera tours for a controlled demonstration;
- provider-proxy patterns, response limits and attribution display.

**REJECT for the Qatar product:** military HUDs, target tracking, detection boxes, sensor effects, public cameras, individual flight/vessel tracking, intelligence jargon, the repository's product name, and any feature that suggests monitoring identifiable workers. These features are irrelevant to Capability-to-Contract and create avoidable trust, procurement and reputational risk.

### Licensing, attribution and commercial-use gate

**VERIFIED:** The repository's `LICENSE` grants MIT rights to the source code and requires preservation of the copyright and permission notice. It expressly excludes third-party data and assets. Its data notice identifies commercial restrictions or separate terms for several bundled/runtime sources, including non-commercial TeleGeography and event imagery, OpenStreetMap/ODbL obligations, provider-specific Google/Cesium terms and separately licensed 3D models. [Repository license](https://github.com/MajorDream444/gods-eye-view/blob/main/LICENSE), [data-source register](https://github.com/MajorDream444/gods-eye-view/blob/main/DATA_SOURCES.md)

**CONFLICT resolved:** GitHub repository metadata reported no recognized SPDX license, while the checked-in `LICENSE` file and `package.json` state MIT. The license file is the controlling artifact for code review, but counsel should still confirm the exact upstream commit and all copied files before commercial release.

Commercial deployment gate:

1. pin the upstream commit and preserve the MIT notice and Bilawal Sidhu attribution;
2. create a software-bill-of-materials and third-party asset/data register;
3. remove all non-commercial datasets, models and imagery unless separately licensed;
4. replace public OSINT feeds with approved Foundry, sponsor or contract-compliant sources;
5. preserve OSM/ODbL and map-provider credits wherever those sources are used;
6. obtain written Hanzo permission for any proprietary Hanzo World code, design system or APIs;
7. perform legal review before using the fork in a paid Qatar pilot.

The MajorDream444 fork does not erase upstream copyright or third-party obligations.

### Mapping, hosting and provider economics

**VERIFIED:** The reviewed engine can start with keyless Esri imagery and OSM fallback. Photorealistic 3D can use Cesium ion or direct Google Map Tiles; place search, traffic and some live layers use additional providers and keys. The repository warns that some free/community options are limited to eligible personal or non-commercial use and that provider terms, quotas and attribution apply. It also treats client-side Google/Cesium credentials as public tokens that must be scope- and URL-restricted. [Repository setup and provider notes](https://github.com/MajorDream444/gods-eye-view), [security model](https://github.com/MajorDream444/gods-eye-view/blob/main/SECURITY.md)

**PROPOSAL:** The prototype should use a flat or restrained 3D Cesium view, OSM-compatible basemap data and sponsor-supplied/fictional overlays. Do not pay for photorealistic 3D until user testing shows that buildings or terrain improve a decision. Avoid live traffic, CCTV, aviation, AIS, satellite and other high-volume feeds in the pilot.

**OPEN cost items:**

- commercial Cesium ion or Google Map Tiles usage and caching rules;
- geocoding/place-search volume;
- Qatar-hosted compute, object storage, logs, backups and disaster recovery;
- commercial support for the chosen map stack;
- data-partner licensing and refresh frequency;
- penetration testing, security review and any customer-required certification.

No reliable total can be quoted until the map provider, monthly active users, tile/session volume, fidelity, data refresh and hosting boundary are fixed. Build the cost model from provider calculators and a capped load test, not a generic per-user assumption.

### Security, privacy and residency

**VERIFIED:** The repository describes itself as a local-first explorer for public data, not a hardened production service. Its useful patterns include server-side credential brokering, short-lived AI session tokens, restricted client keys, fixed-host provider proxies, SSRF controls, response limits, timeouts, sanitized errors and localhost-by-default operation. These are inputs to—not substitutes for—a production security architecture. [Repository security model](https://github.com/MajorDream444/gods-eye-view/blob/main/SECURITY.md)

**PROPOSAL:** Foundry World Qatar should use:

- Qatar-approved hosting when required by the customer or data class;
- tenant isolation, role- and purpose-based access, least privilege and SSO;
- encryption in transit and at rest, managed secrets and key rotation;
- immutable approval/audit records, retention schedules and deletion workflows;
- aggregation thresholds that prevent re-identification of small cohorts;
- pseudonymous participant IDs outside authorized case views;
- no worker location telemetry, inferred movement or public individual scores;
- separate operational, analytics and public-demo datasets;
- model/tool allowlists and human confirmation for consequential actions;
- a data-protection impact assessment and Qatar legal review before real personal or employment data is loaded.

**OPEN:** Exact Qatar residency, cross-border transfer, employment-record, government-cloud and sector-security requirements must be confirmed with the intended buyer and qualified Qatar counsel. National policy alignment alone does not answer the customer's contractual control requirements.

### Three-view data policy

| View | Permitted content | Excluded content |
|---|---|---|
| Public/demo | Official statistics; public program and infrastructure locations; open geographic boundaries; aggregated outcomes above a safe threshold; conspicuously labeled fictional journeys | Names, profiles, exact cohort locations, proprietary supplier status, buyer requirements, evidence documents |
| Authorized sponsor | Contracted cohort/SME records, readiness bands, assigned interventions, milestone state, approved evidence summaries and site/cluster comparisons | Data outside the sponsor's purpose, raw model reasoning, unrelated tenants, unnecessary precise locations |
| Restricted case | Identity, contact data, detailed work evidence, supervisor decisions, commercial documents and correction history for specifically authorized staff | Public/export access by default; broad executive access without purpose |

Every visual mark should carry provenance: `official open data`, `partner supplied`, `Foundry verified`, `participant submitted`, `model derived`, or `simulated`.

### Data sourcing boundary

**VERIFIED open-data candidates:** national and municipal boundaries, public facility/program locations, published infrastructure, official aggregate statistics and properly attributed open-map features. Each dataset still needs a recorded source, date, license, refresh policy and geographic accuracy note.

### Smallest impressive prototype

**PROPOSAL: one journey, two views, one map.** Do not build a national digital twin.

Use a fictional or expressly consented professional-services/logistics-support SME and one role inside it:

1. A sponsor opens an aggregated Qatar map showing one pilot zone and six fictional SMEs as readiness bands, not live dots.
2. Selecting one SME opens its buyer requirement, baseline gap and 12-week pathway.
3. A participant completes one real assignment using a governed HAMAL agent.
4. MobStack routes the output to a human supervisor, records correction and approval, and returns the receipt to Foundry OS.
5. The SME readiness record advances only after approved evidence is attached.
6. The executive view shows the before/after readiness state, elapsed time, accepted deliverable and next qualification step.
7. A public-demo toggle replaces identities and private documents with simulated aggregates; an authorized toggle exposes the consented evidence chain.

Prototype deliverables:

- one Qatar map/cluster screen;
- one SME capability card and gap-to-task workflow;
- one participant assignment with governed-agent disclosure;
- one human-approval and correction receipt;
- one evidence room;
- one before/after executive summary and timeline;
- visible provenance, access level and `SIMULATED` labels.

### Spatial layer go/no-go test

Proceed beyond the prototype only if interviews or pilot observation show at least two of these:

- sponsor decisions differ by site, region or supplier cluster;
- program managers cannot see where capability bottlenecks concentrate;
- projects must match supplier readiness to geographic delivery demand;
- time/location playback exposes execution risk that tables conceal;
- executives understand and act on the spatial view faster than the portfolio view.

Otherwise retain Hanzo World and the visualization engine as future demonstration assets and invest in the Foundry OS workflow, evidence graph, permissions and buyer integrations first.
